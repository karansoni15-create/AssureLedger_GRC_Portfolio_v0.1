# ISMS context and scope — simulated working paper
Document: SC-01 | Version 0.1 | Owner: simulated ISMS lead | Status: draft for exercise

## Organization and service
HarbourCloud Services is a fictional 45-person Australian B2B SaaS company. Staff use M365, Azure and managed laptops to support business customers. The portfolio concerns the people, processes and technology handling support records and service administration. No real organization has approved these assumptions.

## Boundary
Include tenant administration, staff access, support information, endpoints, SaaS procurement, backup/recovery and incident coordination. Include remote workers and outsourced dependencies. Exclude product-source analysis and operation of provider-owned physical facilities from the pilot testing activities; exclusions do not remove responsibility for supplier assurance. Shared-office access must be assessed before retaining the illustrative physical-entry exclusion.

## Interested parties and needs
| Party | Need | Evidence to seek |
|---|---|---|
| Customers | Confidential handling and reliable service | Contracts, access review and restore results |
| Staff | Clear procedures and usable access | Training records, onboarding/offboarding samples |
| Management | Visible exposure and accountable decisions | Risk register and review minutes |
| Suppliers | Defined responsibilities and escalation | Agreements, assurance reports and contact paths |
| Regulators | Applicable obligations addressed | Reviewed obligations register; applicability not assumed |

## Context and climate review
External drivers include customer assurance requests, supply-chain dependency and cloud outages. Internally, a small IT team creates key-person dependency. For the amendment review, consider severe weather and power/network disruption affecting remote staff and provider regions. The simulated preliminary conclusion is that climate-related disruption is relevant to continuity planning. Record the evidence and management decision in a real assessment; no climate-compliance claim is made here.

## Roles and approval boundaries
IT owns access and endpoint treatments. Operations owns recovery. Procurement owns supplier remediation. Risk owners propose treatment and accept residual exposure within delegated authority. Management decides on exposure above appetite and resources. An assessor independent of the action owner verifies closure. In v0.1 these are data fields and exercise roles, not authenticated permissions.

## Objectives and measurement
Proposed quarterly targets: all high/critical risks have accountable treatment owners and dates; no overdue high-risk action without documented escalation; all implemented pilot controls have current reviewed evidence. These are management targets, not achieved outcomes. Review monthly and after material incidents or service changes.
